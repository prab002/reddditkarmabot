My MacBook has 8GB of free space left.

So I did what everyone does. I opened **System Settings → General → Storage** to find out what's eating my disk.

Here's what Apple showed me:

- **Documents:** 48GB
- **macOS:** 39GB
- **Applications:** 17GB
- **System Data:** 130GB

130GB. More than half of my entire 245GB drive.

And it's the one category you can't click into. No list. No files. No explanation. Just a giant grey bar.

Right above it, Apple had a helpful suggestion for me:

**"Store in iCloud."**

## The iCloud trap

Here's the thing: iCloud moves your documents, photos and messages to the cloud.

My documents are 48GB. Even if I moved every single one of them, the 130GB of System Data would still be sitting there, untouched.

So Apple's fix for my full Mac is a monthly subscription that doesn't touch the actual problem.

I don't think it's evil. I think it's the only button Apple built. The Storage screen is great at measuring the problem and terrible at explaining it.

## What "System Data" actually is

System Data isn't a folder. It's a junk drawer. It's everything macOS didn't bother to put a label on:

- **Hidden backups.** When your Time Machine drive isn't plugged in, macOS keeps snapshots on your internal disk instead.
- **Caches.** Browsers, Slack, Spotify, Adobe and Xcode all keep caches that grow for months and never clean themselves up.
- **Leftovers.** Apps you deleted years ago often leave gigabytes of data behind.
- **Old iPhone backups.** Each one can be 10–30GB, even for phones you sold long ago.
- **System temp files and logs** in folders Finder won't even show you.

None of this is your stuff. Most of it is safe to clear. But Apple gives you no way to see it, so you can't.

## Your three "official" options

When your Mac fills up, the choices Apple leaves you are:

1. **Pay for iCloud.** Doesn't fix System Data.
2. **Buy a bigger Mac next time.** Apple charges $200 per storage upgrade.
3. **Erase everything and reinstall macOS.** Hours of setup, and the junk slowly comes back.

All three cost you money or time. None of them tell you what's actually wrong.
