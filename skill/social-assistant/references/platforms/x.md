# X (Twitter)

## How reach works (as commonly observed; it changes, so check memory/LEARNINGS.md)

- **Links in the main post get much less reach.** Post the value in the text or an image,
  then put the link in the **first reply** under your own post, or just in your bio.
- **Replies to bigger accounts are the fastest growth for a small account.** A sharp,
  useful reply under a popular Mac/Apple/tech post gets seen by their audience.
- **The first hour decides it.** Reply to everyone who comments early.
- **Images and short screen recordings** (a before/after storage bar, a 10-second fix)
  beat plain text for how-to content.
- Free accounts get 280 characters per post. Longer content = a thread (numbered posts).

## Formats

**Single post (tip):**
```
<hook: the surprising fact or relatable pain, one line>

<the fix, 1–3 short lines, a command in plain text if needed>
```
Example:
```
Your Mac's "System Data" is 80GB and Apple won't tell you why.

It's usually Time Machine snapshots. One Terminal command:
tmutil listlocalsnapshots /
```

**Thread (3–7 posts):** post 1 is the hook and promise ("How I got 60GB back on a 256GB Mac 🧵"
only if true), each next post is one step, and the last post is a recap plus
"full guide with screenshots: <link> (my site)". The link goes in the last post, not the first.

**Reply to someone else's post:** 1–2 lines, add something they didn't say. Never drop
your link in a reply to a stranger unless they asked for a tool.

## Style

- Plain, punchy, lowercase is fine. No hashtag walls (0–1 hashtags).
- Numbers and specifics ("60GB", "M4 Air", "one command") beat adjectives.
- Your own account = your product is obviously yours; say "I built" not "check out this tool".
